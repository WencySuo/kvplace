# Report figures (GH200 results), in suggested order of appearance

Generated by `results/gh200/report_figs.py` from `results/gh200/results/*.jsonl|csv` and Ellie's
L40S `midterm_16k.jsonl`. PNG (200 dpi) and PDF (vector) for each. Colors: blue / orange / aqua
from a CVD-validated palette; every series is also direct-labeled.

## Fig 1 `fig1_offload_cost_vs_resident` (headline)
ms per output token at 16k vs number of layers kept in HBM, GH200 vs L40S, ordinary and
speculative. **Claim:** on the GH200 the cost of offloading the verifier KV cache nearly vanishes:
speculative decoding is flat at 30-31 ms/token from 0 to 36 resident layers (+3% at full offload),
ordinary decoding costs +53% at full offload and is within 10% once 9 layers are resident. On the
L40S the same offload cost 5x (ordinary) and 2x (speculative). The hollow diamond is the stock
`hbm` DynamicCache arm, which on GH200 is slower than the preallocated all-resident cache for
CPU-side reasons (use R=36 as the HBM reference).
Numbers: GH200 ordinary 61 -> 40 ms/token (R=0 -> 36), speculative 31 -> 30; L40S 179 -> 35, 98 -> 49.

## Fig 2 `fig2_host_read_bandwidth_by_access_pattern` (premise and the catch)
Effective GB/s pulling one 64 MB KV layer from Grace memory by five access patterns. **Claim:**
SM kernels read host memory in place at 332 GB/s, 95% of the copy engine's 350 (the DirectKV
premise holds); single-query attention gets the full 331; but the 16-query verify block through
transformers' `repeat_kv` expansion gets only 50 GB/s (strided 4x re-read) and even fused GQA only
104. The link is not the limit; the access pattern is.

## Fig 3 `fig3_where_the_time_goes` (the CPU bottleneck)
Decode-only profile at 16k: wall step (outline) vs GPU kernel time (fill), split into attention,
GQA expand copy, cache cat, other. **Claim:** ordinary decoding is 10 ms of GPU work inside a
48 ms step (21% busy, 2254 kernel launches at ~20 us of CPU each); the copy pipeline's 6.5 ms of
DMA fully overlaps but its per-layer stream waits make it the slowest wall step; zero-copy's extra
5 ms of in-kernel host reads hides in the idle time. Speculative verification is 70% GPU-bound;
19 of its 45 GPU ms are avoidable (repeat_kv 10.3 + torch.cat 8.6), and zero-copy's loss at
block 16 is the 29 ms expansion copy reading host at ~80 GB/s, not the attention kernel (14.9 ms,
same as HBM).

## Fig 4 `fig4_zerocopy_vs_copy_vs_context`
Step time and TTFT vs context (8k/16k/32k) for stock HBM, copy pipeline R=0, zero-copy R=0.
**Claim:** zero-copy wins ordinary decoding everywhere (39 vs 71 ms at 32k, flat with context) and
ties speculative at 8k, but loses 8% at 16k and 41% at 32k (112 vs 79) because of the expansion
copy in Fig 3; its prefill is 3-4.5x slower because prefill also reads host (fixed in the T2
branch by prefilling in HBM). The stock `hbm` arm's large error bars are prompt-to-prompt variance
of its CPU-side cost.

## Fig 5 `fig5_eviction_budget_vs_step` (eviction policy v1)
Step time vs HBM budget per request for six policies, copy pipeline, 16k. **Claim:** for
speculative decoding a token window is the better eviction unit: keeping the last 2048 tokens of
every layer in HBM (448 MB) gives 56 ms/step, better than half the layers resident (1352 MB,
58-59 ms) at a third of the memory. For ordinary decoding the opposite: resident-*first* layers
(43 ms) beat resident-last (62) and any token window (62-63), because the copy pipeline needs
resident layers' slack to prefetch. The all-resident row is from the 6-prompt sweep; the others
share the same 4 prompts.

## Caveats to state in the report
- Batch size 1, eager transformers 5.15 + DFlash Python loop: the step is CPU/launch-bound, so
  "hidden" costs partly hide behind Python. A GPU-bound engine (CUDA graphs) would expose
  zero-copy's in-kernel reads (est. 1.6x for ordinary decoding) and shrink the headroom.
- The stock `hbm` arm is not a fair HBM baseline on GH200; Ellie's cost model's 44% error is
  mostly from using it as the reference (see task/cost-model: 21% with R=36 as reference).
- Block-size-1 outputs of all offloaded arms differ from `hbm` ~50% of the time (strided KV
  views -> different kernel path); block 16 matches 100%.
